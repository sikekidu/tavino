Who Really Supplies America's Furniture?

US import records (demand side) × China-side identity check (supply side) — HS 9401 (seats) & HS 9403 (other furniture). Episode 2 of the Two-Sided Dossier series.

Furniture is worse than plastics. Of the 8 biggest China/HK “suppliers”, four are parcel consolidators holding ~86% of the bill volume, two are trading arms, and only two are real factories. The #1 name alone outweighs the other seven combined.
The demand side reads clean — IKEA, Rooms To Go, Costco. The supply side is where the shells live.

1 · The US side: real retailers, one ghost

HS 9401 totals 5,150,814 lifetime sea shipments, 60.5% from China; HS 9403 totals 11,348,146, 52.5% from China (ImportYeti, database updated 18 Nov 2026). The importer lists are dominated by genuine retailers — IKEA Supply AG, RTG Furniture (Rooms To Go), American Furniture Warehouse, Costco — the opposite of the plastics chapters. Yet even here, the #1 “importer” of subheading 9401.80 is CrimsonLogic US Inc, a trade-compliance software firm that appears as consignee on other people's filings. And in that same subheading, the top four “suppliers” are all parcel consolidators — 48% of the entire subheading's bills.

3 · Three spotlights

The kilogram test Case A

One table line settles the whole episode. Zauba's China-exporter table for HS 9403.20 (metal furniture) ranks August Amber #1 with 70,744 shipments totalling 183 metric tons — ≈2.6 kg per “metal furniture” shipment — and Fujian Dav Technologies #2 with 4,263 shipments totalling 73,800 metric tons — ≈17.3 t each. Same code, same ranking, a 400-fold weight gap. A bill of lading counts a 2 kg parcel and a 17-ton container as equals; weight doesn't lie. Any supplier ranking published without a weight column is, in this heading, mostly a ranking of parcel shells.

General Toys — the buyer wearing a supplier costume Case B

A “toys” company in a Tsim Sha Tsui office tower is a top-5 US furniture “supplier” — because 96% of its lifetime FCL volume (16,500 of 17,172 shipments) goes to a single consignee, Goplus Corp, the Costway e-commerce seller. Its cargo originates from factories in at least five countries (Vietnam, Singapore, China, Malaysia, Hong Kong). In substance it is Goplus's own Hong Kong export desk: the buyer's shadow printed on the shipper line. Counting it as an independent supplier double-counts the buyer as its own source.

Zoy Home — the real factory ranks fifth Case C

Zoy Home Furnishing is Zhongyuan Home (SSE: 603709), founded 2001 in Anji — China's chair county — with three plants over 450,000 m² and ~1,500 containers/month of capacity. Its record is textbook: 22,132 US shipments since 2015, 100% FCL, ≈7.4 t per shipment, customers including Costco and American Furniture Warehouse. A genuine listed manufacturer — and it still ranks only #5 among China/HK shippers, behind three parcel shells and a trading desk. That ordering is the market's information problem in one line.

4 · The method, updated: add the kilogram test

Episode 1's five signals (parcel signature, product concentration, consignee shape, own SCAC/house bills, registered name) all held. Furniture adds the decisive sixth:

  • Weight per bill — divide total tonnage by bill count. Factories ship tonnes per bill; parcel shells ship kilograms. Below ~50 kg average in a furniture heading, it is not furniture trade — it is a parcel stream wearing a furniture label.
  • One-customer test — if >90% of a “supplier's” volume lands at one consignee, you are looking at that buyer's own offshore desk, not a market supplier.

6 · What episode 2 confirms — and its limits

Confirms: the pattern is not a plastics quirk. In the largest China-advantage category of all, bill-count rankings are dominated by parcel logistics, and the fixes are mechanical: weight per bill, consignee concentration, FCL/LCL split. The two-sided dossier format replicates with the same free sources and the same identity work.

Limits: ImportYeti's ranking tables are JavaScript-rendered, so top-10 lists here are aggregated from ImportDoor's five largest subheadings per heading (~93–95% coverage, Jun 2018 – Oct 2026 window) — a different base from Episode 1's, kept separate throughout. Two consolidators' registered Chinese names could not be confirmed from public English sources; their verdicts rest on shipment physics, which is unambiguous. Rankings remain lifetime/window bill counts, not annual value.
