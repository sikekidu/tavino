The #1 "Chinese Supplier" of US Lighting Is a Thai Auto-Parts Factory

US import records (demand side) × China-side identity check (supply side) — HS 9405 (luminaires and lighting fittings). Episode 4 of the Two-Sided Dossier series.

The #1 “Chinese supplier” of US lighting is a Thai auto-trim factory. Of the eight biggest names on the bill-count leaderboard, the two real factories are in Thailand and Vietnam — and no identified Chinese household-lighting factory appears in the top eight at all. What is left on the China side is a parcel shell, a US 3PL's Shenzhen arm, a general trader and two unverified Guangdong names. In lighting, the leaderboard measures offshore production and logistics, not Chinese manufacturing.
The US buyer list is the most recognisable of any episode — IKEA alone holds 4,522 bills. The shell still wins a whole subheading outright: August Amber tops glass lighting parts with 906 bills, its third crown in four episodes.

1 · The US side: brands, big-box retail, and three odd names

HS 9405 totals 1,516,727 lifetime sea shipments (ImportYeti, database updated 18 Nov 2026). The importer list is the most recognisable of any episode: IKEA SUPPLY AG 4,522 bills across seven subheadings (including 1,622 table/desk/floor lamps alone), KICHLER LIGHTING 1,618, HOME DEPOT 1,328 (family total 1,928 with its two sibling entities), COOPER LIGHTING SOLUTIONS 1,307, PLASTIC TRIM INTERNATIONAL 1,044 across its two entities, CURREY AND COMPANY 770, and SIGNIFY NORTH AMERICA (the Philips lighting arm) in two subheadings. Three quirks: STRYKER COMMUNICATIONS INC is the #2 importer of LED chandeliers (315 bills) — surgical lighting, supplied by Germany's Berchtold; HELLA AUTOMOTIVE MEXICO is the #3 importer of plastic lighting parts (452) — car lighting filed under the furniture chapter; and 5100 COM recurs across five subheadings (893 bills) — identity unverified, the same opaque name that appears in the auto-parts episode.

3 · Three spotlights

IKEA is the story, not a name on the list Case A

No supplier in this episode matters as much as one buyer. IKEA SUPPLY AG holds 4,522 bills — more than any three suppliers combined — spread across seven of the fifteen subheadings, and its supply chain is visible bill by bill: Huizhou SDG Tonsun → IKEA, 259 bills; Taicang New Brite → IKEA, 159; Foshan Yunlu Lighting Factory → IKEA, 154. Three factories whose entire US export business, as recorded, is one customer. For an importer, that is the real lesson of the lighting data: the leaderboard's biggest buyer is a single retailer with captive lanes, and the factories behind it are not on the open market in any meaningful sense. The “supplier” you can call is rarely the one shipping the most.

August Amber's third crown — and it owns the glass Case B

August Amber HK Limited is the #1 supplier of glass lighting parts (HS 940591) with 906 bills — ahead of Currey International's 437 and a real crystal-arts factory, Baoying Qili, at 187. It is the same name that topped HS 3926 plastics in Episode 1 (≈4.6% of the whole category) and HS 9403.20 furniture in Episode 2 (29.2% of that subheading), and the same 2–3 kg-per-bill signature reproduces across six sampled HS codes (2.1–2.8 kg each). The companion ghost, JENNY, holds 110 bills here and 4,317 in the adjacent HS 853990 (lamp parts) — #1 there. In lighting, the shells are outnumbered by real trade — but they still win the one subheading where the product is small, light and shippable by the parcel.

The subheading that is secretly car lighting Case C

HS 940592 — “plastic parts of luminaires” — has nothing to do with home lighting. Its top importer is Plastic Trim International (559 + 485 bills), its top supplier is Minth Asia Pacific (1,135), and the second-strongest lane is COKO Werk → Hella Automotive Mexico (436), with TCL Moka, Inteva and IAC in the importer list — all automotive names, all carried over from Episode 3's auto-parts episode. The origin mix gives it away too: Thailand 1,158 and Germany 872 sit beside China, the only subheading where Germany cracks the top three. A buyer who reads “lighting parts” and starts calling chandelier factories would be dialling the wrong industry entirely.

4 · The method, updated: filter by origin before ranking

Episodes 1–2's signals (parcel signature, product concentration, consignee shape, own SCAC/house bills, registered name) all held — with two refinements this episode:

  • Filter by origin before ranking, or the table imports the wrong countries. ImportDoor's top-10 supplier tables do not show origin per supplier, so a naive “top China suppliers” ranking pulls in a Thai plant, a Vietnamese factory, a German moulder and a Philippines captive arm. This episode's ranking is filtered to mainland China + Hong Kong across all 15 subheadings (Taiwan excluded, noted separately) — and even then the #1 name is Thai. The naive top-8-subheading ranking (Minth, August Amber, Currey, Century, Pacific, Pro Tang, Macron, Coko) is not used.
  • The two bases diverge by an order of magnitude. ImportYeti counts 1,516,727 lifetime sea shipments for HS 9405; ImportDoor counts 84,262 bill-of-lading records for the same heading (and 788,155 vs 5,150,814 for HS 9401 in Episode 2). The gap is a coverage/definition difference, not an error in either source — so the headline total comes only from ImportYeti and every share and ranking only from ImportDoor, never crossed. Within ImportDoor, China-origin bills are 58.2% of the heading (49,028 of 84,262).
  • HS 2022 split: ImportDoor uses HS 2022 codes for this heading. The old codes 940510/940520/940530/940540/940560 have no product pages (404), and bills filed before ~2022 under them are missing from ImportDoor's 84,262 — its total is a lower bound with uneven per-page windows. ImportYeti's 1,516,727 is the only full-coverage figure.
  • Subheading identity matters more than heading identity. HS 9405 contains three different trades — home lighting, surgical lighting (Berchtold → Stryker) and car lighting (Minth → Plastic Trim). The heading total alone would blend them.

6 · What episode 4 confirms — and its limits

Confirms: the shell pattern is category-dependent and product-dependent — and the “China” in a supplier ranking is not always China. Where the product is industrial and the buyers are brands (lighting), the leaderboard is mostly real trade — but the real trade is offshore: a Thai auto-trim plant, a Vietnamese luminaire factory, a German headlamp moulder, a Philippines captive arm. The biggest story is a single buyer's captive supply chain (IKEA, 4,522 bills), not a supplier at all. And the shell still wins wherever the product is small and parcel-shippable: glass parts, where August Amber takes the subheading outright. The kilogram test reproduces across a fourth heading.

Limits: two of the top eight suppliers (Extra Light, Hestia) are classified as not identity-checked on bill-pattern evidence only — no plant evidence was found, and their verdicts may change with better sources. The ImportDoor/ImportYeti base gap (84,262 vs 1,516,727) is unresolved as a definition question; all shares in this episode are ImportDoor-internal. The HS 2022 split means pre-2022 bills under the old codes are missing from ImportDoor's total. 5100 COM (893 bills across five subheadings) is unresolved. August Amber's 940591-native kilogram slice was not retrievable; the 2–3 kg signature is established across six other HS codes and in Episodes 2–3, and is code-independent — but a 940591-native slice would strengthen the episode.
