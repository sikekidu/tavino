Who Really Supplies America's Plastic Goods?

US import records (demand side) × China-side identity check (supply side) — HS 3925 & 3926, US sea bills of lading, Jan 2015 – Nov 2026. Episode 1 of the Two-Sided Dossier series.

Of the 9 biggest China-side “suppliers” in these two chapters, 8 are logistics shells — freight forwarders, NVOCCs and e-commerce parcel fronts. Exactly one is a real factory.
Raw importer/supplier lists rank the middlemen. The value is in digging underneath them — on both sides of the Pacific.

1 · The US side: buyers buried under forwarders

In HS 3926 (other articles of plastics, 11.9M lifetime sea shipments), the #1 “importer” is Expeditors International (48,898 shipments) — a freight forwarder that buys nothing. Four of the top 7 consignees are logistics firms. In HS 3925 (builders' ware of plastics, 333,509 shipments) the list reads cleaner — Home Depot USA (4,943) and Springs Window Fashions (4,613) are real end buyers — because the category is concentrated around window treatments. Same database, opposite readability: category structure decides whether raw lists are usable at all.

3 · Three spotlights

August Amber — the veil over 4.6% of a category Case A

One Hong Kong name sits on 549,261 bills in HS 3926 alone — 4.6% of an 11.9M-shipment category — and 4,199,045 US shipments all-time (ImportGenius). Its address is a unit in Lippo Centre, Admiralty. The bills give it away: 1–3 kg, one package each, products with nothing in common (storage racks, football shoes, photo albums, golf clubs), consignees who are individuals at residential addresses from Chicago to Port Huron, carrier Apollo Logistics (ALHS). This is the shipper-of-record for mass small-parcel e-commerce. Behind the name stand thousands of anonymous sellers — and any “top supplier” ranking that counts it is measuring a logistics veil, not supply capacity.

Hainan Golden Shell — half a million “customers” Case B

ImportDoor counts 739,102 bills shipped to 515,214 distinct US customers — whose top names are private individuals listed by first name (“MARIA”, 155 shipments). A real trading company has dozens of buyers; a parcel network has half a million doorsteps. Top product lines are whatever its sellers ship: radiators, storage boxes, lavatory seats, trailers. Same verdict as Case A, second-largest name in the chapter.

Beautiful Window — the real one, and how you can tell Case C

The exception proves the method. Beautiful Window Enterprise ships one product family — blinds and shutters — in full containers (4,719 shipments, 10,132 TEU since 2012), ranks #1 supplier of HS 392530 to the US (904 shipments), and sells to the actual industry: Springs Window Fashions (the #2 importer in HS 3925), Mariak, Elegant Windows. It runs its own plants in Taicang, Jiangsu and Bavet, Cambodia, exporting through a Hong Kong arm. Every signal points the opposite way from the eight shells above.

4 · The method: five signals that separate factories from veils

  • Parcel signature — thousands of 1–6 kg house bills = parcel consolidator, whatever the name says.
  • Product concentration — one HS family at FCL weight = maker; a dozen unrelated chapters = intermediary.
  • Consignee shape — named businesses in the trade vs. individuals at home addresses vs. the shipper's own affiliates.
  • Carrier paperwork — an own SCAC code and house bills mean it issues transport documents: an NVOCC, by definition.
  • The registered name — Chinese registry entries don't hide it: “…International Cargo Agent Co., Ltd.” is a forwarder wearing a supplier costume.

6 · What this episode proves — and its limits

Proves: the two-sided join works with free public data plus identity work. One analyst pass turned a raw “top suppliers” list into a verified map: who is a veil, who is an agent for which retail programs, and who actually makes the product — the layer neither US data platforms (raw records) nor China-side directories (unverified listings) provide.

Limits: rankings are lifetime (2015–2026), not annual; free pages show top-N only; name variants split counts across databases; the 1688 domestic price band and per-buyer shipment rhythm require the deeper pull. None of these limits affect the verdicts above.
