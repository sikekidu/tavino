The #1 "Chinese Supplier" of US Auto Parts Is Advance Auto Parts Itself

US import records (demand side) × China-side identity check (supply side) — HS 8708 (parts and accessories of motor vehicles). Episode 3 of the Two-Sided Dossier series.

Auto parts inverts the pattern. Of the 8 biggest China/HK “suppliers”, six are real factories holding 61% of the bill volume, the #1 name is Advance Auto Parts' own offshore buying desk shipping 19-tonne containers — and the only parcel shell in the list holds just 13.7%. For once, the bill-count leaderboard is mostly the real trade.
The demand side is OEMs and Tier-1s, not retailers. The one familiar ghost still shows up — and its signature is identical to the third decimal.

1 · The US side: OEMs, Tier-1s, and three ghosts

HS 8708 totals 7,609,442 lifetime sea shipments (ImportYeti, database updated 18 Nov 2026). China is far less dominant here than in furniture: 25.6% of bill count across the eleven largest subheadings (ImportDoor, Jun 2018 – Oct 2026), and only the #1 origin in 8 of 11 — in the two largest subheadings (other parts, body parts) it is #4 and a bare #1. The importer list reads like an industry directory — Brose, ZF, Dana, Benteler, Ford, Continental, Mercedes-Benz Vans, Stellantis, Bosch. Three quirks: Vestas Nacelles America is the #2 importer of “gear boxes” — wind-turbine gearboxes filed under the auto-parts heading; Walmart imports drive-axles (1,195 bills); and a helicopter company, Ligon Helicopter Corp, is the #4 importer of mufflers — identity unverified.

3 · Three spotlights

Crossroads — the buyer wearing a supplier costume, at scale Case A

The #1 “supplier” of US auto parts makes nothing. Crossroads Global Trade is Advance Auto Parts' own offshore buying desk — proven not by inference but by an SEC filing: Advance Auto Global Sourcing, Inc. was renamed Crossroads Global Trading Corp. in December 2008. Its bills run from a Shanghai bonded warehouse, its goods originate in five countries, and 94.2% of its 28,550 lifetime shipments go to companies inside the Advance group (Advance Stores, CQ Sourcing, Advance Auto Parts). It is Episode 2's General Toys (Goplus's Hong Kong shell) at far greater scale, with the buyer's identity on the public record. The lesson for any importer reading a supplier ranking: the biggest name on the list may be your competitor's procurement arm, not a factory you can call.

August Amber's signature, reproduced to the decimal Case B

Zauba's HS 870880 exporter table contains one China row with 969 shipments totalling 2.5 metric tons — ≈2.6 kg per “suspension” shipment. The count matches ImportDoor's named August Amber row for the same code exactly, and 2.6 kg is the identical figure Episode 2 measured for the same company under HS 9403.20 (70,744 shipments / 183 t). Three headings, one signature. The shell that dominated furniture (133,443 bills) is present in auto parts too — but here it is a bit player, 3,101 bills against Crossroads' 5,747, and its two subheading footholds (mufflers, suspension) are the only places the parcel pattern reaches the top 10 at all.

The Shandong brake cluster Case C

Three of the top eight are brake-disc makers from one Shandong cluster — Winhere (Yantai), Longji (Longkou), and Conmet Xin just below the cut — and the US aftermarket's rotor supply runs through them. Winhere has been a known US-market rotor supplier since the 2007 antidumping review (0.04% de minimis margin); Longji is listed (SZSE 002363) and ships at exactly 1.0 TEU per bill in the cleanest monthly slice. The price anchor: passenger-car cast-iron discs list at $5–15 per piece at MOQ 50–100 on Alibaba export listings; heavy-truck discs $27–45. A buyer who finds this cluster has found the lane, not just a vendor.

4 · The method, updated: bills span containers — the test works at order of magnitude

Episodes 1–2's signals (parcel signature, product concentration, consignee shape, own SCAC/house bills, registered name) all held — with one refinement this episode:

  • Weight per bill is an order-of-magnitude test, not a precise one. Bills in this lane routinely span multiple containers (Conmet Xin: 90 bills / 800 TEU = 8.9 TEU per bill in Aug 2022), so several real shippers average over a single container's payload — Sensen ≈52.8 t, Winhere ≈37.9 t, Longji ≈29.1 t. The kilogram test still discriminates cleanly: the smallest real shipper (Tenneco Suzhou, ≈5.3 t) is ~2,000× August Amber's 2.6 kg. Kilograms versus tonnes is the verdict; the exact tonne figure is not comparable across shippers without TEU data.
  • Declaration quirk: brake rotors also file under HS 841199 (turbine parts) — Crossroads' #1 code overall. The 870830 totals understate the rotor lane; the rotor report (36,589 bills) is the cleaner view of that product.

6 · What episode 3 confirms — and its limits

Confirms: the shell pattern is category-dependent, not universal. Where the product is industrial and the buyers are OEMs (auto parts), the leaderboard is mostly real trade — six factories, one buyer desk, one shell. The two-sided method still earns its keep: without the identity check, the #1 name on the list would read as a supplier you could call, when it is in fact Advance Auto Parts buying from itself. And the kilogram test reproduces across a third heading, down to the same 2.6 kg.

Limits: no ImportYeti-basis China share for HS 8708 — the country-filtered page returns the unfiltered total in text fetch, so the heading's China share is reported only from ImportDoor (25.6% of top-11 bills). Sensen's 123k t Zauba weight is flagged as plausibly inflated; its verdict rests on company-side evidence, not the weight figure. Two name matches are exact but not spelled identically (Crossroads “Trading” vs bill “Trade”; ImportDoor's truncated “Hubei Henglong Automotive System Gr”), stated in the files rather than silently normalised. Ligon Helicopter Corp (#4 muffler importer) and the mangled “Sheng Wang Auto Parts K USA N Co Ltd” are unresolved.
